# Study 01: Techno-Economic & Biophysical Feasibility of Engineered PETase for Industrial Plastic Depolymerization

## Executive Summary
This study assesses whether an engineered plastic-degrading enzyme (FAST-PETase, *Nature* 2022) with a reported 2.5-fold increase in laboratory catalytic activity and a 19.0 °C elevation in melting temperature ($T_m = 67.8\text{ °C}$) can achieve commercial parity with thermochemical recycling (glycolysis/methanolysis) or virgin PET production.

### Core Findings
1. **The Laboratory-to-Factory Disconnect:** While FAST-PETase rapidly depolymerizes low-crystallinity ($< 5\%$) amorphous PET film at 50 °C, post-consumer PET bottles have crystallinities of $30\%–40\%$. Above $15\%$ crystallinity, depolymerization velocity drops by $> 85\%$.
2. **Pre-treatment Cost Dominance:** Thermal extrusion and rapid quenching to amorphize post-consumer waste adds $\$0.32–\$0.45/kg$ in CAPEX and electrical power, wiping out the enzymatic cost advantage.
3. **Enzyme Synthesis Burden:** At an enzyme loading of $3\text{ mg enzyme} / g\text{ PET}$, a $50,000\text{ MT/yr}$ recycling facility requires $150\text{ MT}$ of pure enzyme per year. At an *E. coli* fermentation cost of $\$25/kg$ enzyme, the biocatalyst alone contributes $\$0.075/kg$ PET.
4. **Verdict:** **CONDITIONAL HOLD / REJECT PILOT FUNDING**. Do not allocate capital to pilot bioreactor construction until:
   - Enzyme half-life at $65\text{ °C}$ exceeds 72 hours in continuous stirred slurry.
   - Depolymerization of $25\%$ crystalline PET exceeds $80\%$ without pre-treatment amorphization.

## Directory Structure
- `question.md`: Formal research decision and diligence questions.
- `methods.md`: Biophysical and chemical engineering methodology.
- `sources.json`: Primary peer-reviewed citations and PDB accessions.
- `evidence.csv`: Extracted experimental observations with error margins.
- `assumptions.yaml`: All model assumptions explicitly classified by data source.
- `calculations.py`: Deterministic executable mass-balance and economic model.
- `figures/`: Vector plots and sensitivity curves generated by `calculations.py`.
- `results/`: Output JSON data and summaries.
- `limitations.md`: Known boundaries, confounding variables, and falsification criteria.
- `verification.json`: Software versions, checksums, and verification timestamps.
