# Research Question & Decision Specification

## 1. Primary Research Question
Does the 2.5-fold laboratory activity enhancement and $19.0\text{ °C}$ thermal stabilization ($T_m = 67.8\text{ °C}$) of FAST-PETase (*Nature* 2022) justify a $\$2.5\text{M}$ capital allocation to build a $500\text{ L}$ enzymatic plastic depolymerization pilot plant, or does substrate crystallinity and enzyme cost render the process economically uncompetitive with chemical recycling?

## 2. Decision Framework
- **Decision Maker:** Venture Investment Committee & Industrial Biotech Corporate Development Team.
- **Alternatives Considered:**
  1. **Option A (Invest):** Fund pilot plant using FAST-PETase biocatalysis.
  2. **Option B (Alternative Biocatalyst):** Pivot to thermophilic LCC-ICCG (*Nature* 2020) operating at $72\text{ °C}$.
  3. **Option C (Chemical Benchmark):** Partner with or invest in thermochemical glycolysis (e.g. Loop Industries / Eastman chemical glycolysis).
  4. **Option D (Reject / Pass):** Cease diligence due to unbridgeable thermodynamic/mass-balance constraints.

## 3. Key Hypotheses to Test
1. **Hypothesis 1 (Thermal Viability):** Operating at $50\text{ °C}$ allows sufficient polymer chain mobility to depolymerize commercial PET without pre-treatment amorphization.
2. **Hypothesis 2 (Enzyme Cost Threshold):** At $3\text{ mg/g}$ enzyme loading and $\$25/kg$ recombinant enzyme production cost, the biocatalyst expense does not exceed $10\%$ of recycled terephthalic acid (rTPA) market value ($\sim \$1.30/kg$).
3. **Hypothesis 3 (Competitiveness):** Total enzymatic operating cost (OPEX) + amortized CAPEX is lower than chemical glycolysis ($\sim \$1.05/kg$ TPA).
