# Limitations, Confounders & Falsification Criteria

## 1. Known Analytical Boundaries
1. **Simplified Kinetic Mass Transfer:** The degradation model assumes uniform enzyme slurry dispersion and Michaelis-Menten kinetics. In reality, post-consumer flakes sediment and create non-Newtonian high-solids slurries ($> 20\text{ wt}\%$) that elevate mixing power requirements.
2. **Product Inhibition:** Hydrolysis products (terephthalic acid and mono-(2-hydroxyethyl) terephthalate, MHET) cause competitive and uncompetitive product inhibition at concentrations $> 15\text{ g/L}$, potentially requiring continuous in situ crystallization or membrane diafiltration.
3. **Enzyme Recycling Not Modeled:** The baseline model conservatively assumes single-use enzyme disposal. If $> 70\%$ of active enzyme can be ultrafiltered and recycled across consecutive batches, the biocatalyst cost contribution decreases from $\$0.105/kg$ to $\$0.032/kg$ TPA.

## 2. Confounding Factors in Literature Extrapolations
- **Amorphous Film vs Bottle Flake:** Lu et al. (*Nature* 2022) reported rapid degradation on solvent-cast or thermally amorphized thin films. Extrapolating these rates to commercial beverage bottle flake ($X_c \approx 34\%$) without pre-treatment amorphization represents a critical category error.
- **Buffer vs Water Systems:** Academic literature universally employs $100\text{ mM}$ sodium phosphate or Tris buffer. Commercial bioprocesses cannot afford stoichiometric buffering salts and must use automated caustic titration ($\text{NaOH}$ or $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$), which alters ionic strength and enzyme precipitation kinetics.

## 3. Explicit Falsification Criteria
The conclusion of this study (**REJECT PILOT FUNDING**) would be falsified and reversed if:
1. An engineered enzyme variant achieves $\ge 80\%$ depolymerization conversion of post-consumer PET flake ($X_c \ge 30\%$) at $55\text{ °C}$ within 24 hours without thermal amorphization.
2. A continuous extrusion pre-treatment method is demonstrated with an all-in capital and operating cost $< \$0.08/kg$ PET.
3. Recombinant enzyme production costs in *E. coli* or *P. pastoris* reach $< \$5.00/kg$ pure protein.
